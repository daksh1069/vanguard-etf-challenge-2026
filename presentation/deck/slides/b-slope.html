Rate cuts: the curve-slope arithmetic

Euro, all-issuer curve

Hedged 2-year ≈ local 2-year + (US overnight − euro overnight)

= 3.13% + (3.88% − 2.44%) = 4.57%, against the US 2-year at 4.46%: a gap of +0.11

= euro slope (3.13 − 2.44 = 0.69) − US slope (4.46 − 3.88 = 0.58)

Canada

= 3.17% + 1.36% = 4.53%, against 4.46%: a gap of +0.07

A Fed cut lowers the hedge carry and US yields together, so the gap stays the difference in slopes. This ignores the basis and trading costs.

Inputs: team rate file, early November 2026 (FRED and national sources). Our calculation

Backup. Use if a judge pushes on “what happens when the Fed cuts?” A dollar investor’s hedged foreign 2-year yield is roughly the local 2-year plus the gap between US and local overnight rates. Subtract the US 2-year and the overnight rates cancel into the two curve slopes. So a Fed cut lowers the hedge carry, but it lowers US yields too, and the fund’s edge over US 2-years is the slope difference. Today the euro curve is a little steeper than the US curve, so the hedged euro 2-year is about 0.1 above the US 2-year. These figures ignore the cross-currency basis, which has helped dollar hedgers, and trading costs.
