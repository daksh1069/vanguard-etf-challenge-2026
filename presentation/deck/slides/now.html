About 50 seconds. Why now? Because central banks are moving apart. The Fed is at about 3.9%. The Bank of England raised to 4% just last week. The Bank of Japan has raised twice this year, to 1.25%. The ECB sits at 2.5%. When rate cycles diverge like this, a second set of short curves is worth more to a US bond investor. And the obvious follow-up: what if the Fed cuts? The hedge carry falls, but US yields fall with it. Against US two-year Treasuries, the fund wins or loses by the difference in curve slopes, not by the rate gap. Right now a euro two-year hedged into dollars yields about 4.57%, against 4.46% for the US two-year. (Backup slide “Rate cuts: the curve-slope arithmetic” has the working if a judge wants it.)
