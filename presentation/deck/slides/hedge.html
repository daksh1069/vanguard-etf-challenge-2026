Forwards: team Bloomberg file, month-end 1M forward points. Rate gaps: OECD 3-month rates. Test A: last 5 years

About 55 seconds. A question we expect: doesn’t hedging cost money? For a US investor, it has paid. The hedge earns roughly the US rate minus the foreign rate, and US rates were mostly higher. On top of that, real forward prices paid dollar hedgers more than the simple rate gap. For the yen, about half a point a year more; for the euro, more than a quarter of a point. We also ran a direct test on Bloomberg: hedged 3-month foreign bills against US T-bills, after fees, over the last five years. Across five countries, the hedged bills came out about 10 to 13 basis points a year ahead. For Germany, the UK and Canada alone it’s about break-even, and most of the gain came from the yen. (If asked: crossing the full quoted spot spread at every roll would turn that test negative, which is why large funds roll hedges through FX swaps.)
