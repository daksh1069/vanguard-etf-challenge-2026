How it works

A bond fund with a monthly currency hedge

1. You buy in USD

A US-listed ETF share, priced in dollars.

2. Fund buys bonds

About 190 short government bonds in 12 currencies.

3. Sells FX forward

1-month forwards lock in today’s rate on 100% of the foreign value.

4. Monthly reset

Forwards settle in cash and roll to match the new holdings.

Repeats every month

What the investor earns, roughly

Foreign short-bond return + hedge carry − 0.10% fee

Hedge carry ≈ US short rate − foreign short rate, plus the cross-currency basis. The currency move itself cancels out.

Same mechanism Vanguard uses for BNDX. Fee: our proposal

About 65 seconds. How it works, in four steps. One: you buy a normal US-listed ETF share, in dollars. Two: the fund buys about 190 short government bonds in twelve currencies. Three, the key step: it sells those currencies one month forward, locking in today’s exchange rate on the whole foreign value. Four: at month end the forwards settle in cash, and the fund rolls into new ones that match its new holdings. Then it repeats. So if the euro falls 5%, the bonds lose 5% in dollar terms, and the forward gains about the same 5%. The currency move cancels. What’s left is at the bottom: the foreign short-bond return, plus the hedge carry, minus the fee. The hedge carry is roughly the US short rate minus the foreign short rate. When US rates are higher, as they’ve been for most of the last decade, hedging adds return instead of costing it.
